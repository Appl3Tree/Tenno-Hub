### Expected TTK objective (2026-11-12)

Added a persisted `Expected TTK` objective that screens legal builds with the existing fast sustained score, then evaluates finite-encounter finalists and up to four passes of legal replacements. The current shortlist allows up to 40 proposals and reserves effect-family coverage before selecting second candidates from any family. Selection compares finite modeled kill times directly: a kill inside the 600-second simulation window outranks a capped encounter, shorter modeled TTK wins between kills, and expected damage per second ranks candidates when none kill. Rank and weapon-configuration comparisons use the same ordering. The explanation keeps the proxy score separate from encounter TTK and discloses mean-field status rolls and the bounded local search. Incarnon path screening now uses finite encounter ranking for Expected TTK, then re-optimizes up to two promising paths. Arcane selection alternates with legal mod refinement for up to three passes. These searches remain bounded and do not establish global joint path/mod/Arcane optimality.

### Nukor Microwave unique status (2026-11-12)

The current [Nukor](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Nukor) and [Kuva Nukor](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Kuva_Nukor) pages document their invisible Microwave status, which persists on struck enemies and counts as a unique status for [Galvanized Shot](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Galvanized_Shot) and [Condition Overload](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Condition_Overload_%28Mechanic%29). Microwave is not an ordinary damage-type proc. Added this effect to the Normal Attack metadata for both weapons, carried it through selected-attack resolution, and included it in the active unique-status count used by the steady-state calculation and finite encounter. The finite encounter adds Microwave after the first attack, so that hit receives no retroactive bonus and later hits can use the status count. The clean-target one-attack calculation explicitly uses `targetStatusState: initial` and leaves the count at zero. This is separate from the optimizer's `Initial` conditional mode, which disables weapon-triggered conditional bonuses but continues to model target status progression in its pool-progress objective.

The Mod Builder exposes the active `microwave` status and its Galvanized/Condition Overload count. Regressions check both weapon variants, clean-target exclusion, steady-state status counting, the subsequent-hit bonus in a finite synthetic encounter, and optimizer scoring across Initial versus Realistic weapon-condition states. Port-80 in-app-browser QA showed the Nukor `UNIQUE STATUS: microwave` row and `Galvanized / CO count` label; reload restored the no-weapon, empty planner without creating a saved build. Full `npm test` passes 548 tests across 48 files; `npm run build` (including `tsc -b`) passes and emits a 59,880-byte optimizer Worker. Source validation, focused ESLint, and CRLF-aware diff checking pass. Other non-damage unique statuses, special immunity exceptions, and cross-weapon status histories remain unsupported; the overall assignment remains open.

### Primary Overcharge energy-aware model (2026-11-12)

The current [Primary Overcharge](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Primary_Overcharge) entry says the rank-based Arcane activates at or above 90% Energy and converts 35% of Max Energy into Multishot, capped at 100%–350%. The catalog now derives that formula and its six rank caps from the current item data. The planner accepts explicit Max Energy and current Energy percentage, applies the effect to stats and optimizer scoring, persists the values in saved builds, and includes them in exported build JSON. Unknown values fail closed: the selected effect is not calculated and Auto Optimize ignores the energy-scaled Arcane until both inputs exist. Energy is held static; draining, regeneration, and energy-restoring proc histories are not simulated.

Port-80 QA verified rank 5 at 1,000 Max Energy / 100% current Energy contributes +350.0% Multishot, while 89% current Energy removes the bonus. After clearing the manual selection, Optimize Arcane selected rank-5 Primary Overcharge; reload restored the empty planner. The Arcane catalog and optimizer tests cover rank caps, threshold, scaling, missing inputs, and candidate selection. A cap-equivalent re-run of the Braton comparison modeled rank-5 Primary Overcharge at 1,000/100 on both lists: Tenno-Hub proxy 487,299.11 / expected TTK 0.824 s; Underframe list proxy 180,414.79 / expected TTK 0.769 s. Underframe's native 105,867 DPS / 0.80 s TTK and Tenno-Hub's 109,013 expected DPS / 0.769 s are close for this fixture, though the slider-to-energy mapping is an explicit assumption. The proxy/TTK ranking disagreement remains an optimizer quality issue.

## Phase 1 — Existing implementation

Pre-change baseline on 2026-11-10: `npm test` passed 335 tests in 39 files; `npm run build` passed. Vite reported its existing large-chunk warning. The command `npm test -- --reporter=dot` also emitted npm's warning that `--reporter` is an unknown npm config before Vitest ran.

The page in `src/pages/tools/ModBuilder.tsx` calls the pure calculation code in `src/domain/logic/damageCalc.ts` and synchronously calls the beam-search optimizer in `src/domain/logic/buildOptimizer.ts`. It has eight general mod slots, an Exilus slot, one weapon arcane, saved builds, owned-mod restrictions, Forma/Catalyst options, progenitor bonuses, and Incarnon evolution selection. The user-facing goals are Burst, Scaling, Crit, and Status.

Key findings:

- The four goals share an approximate score with hand-weighted status, area, and utility terms. The score includes target-independent utility coefficients and assumptions such as two nearby Blast targets. “Crit” optimizes a score preference, not a player outcome.
- Conditional effects use fixed aiming, hit, kill, and headshot uptime estimates; kill-triggered conditions can be treated as fully stacked. Scaling also assumes many conditions are fully active.
- Faction choices map to synthetic armor, health, and shield mixes instead of a selected enemy and level. The model retains legacy `healthMaterial`, `armorMaterial`, and `shieldMaterial` fields, although Damage 3.0 no longer uses those material classes for the faction damage table.
- The optimizer evaluates a candidate, then has post-search paths that can drop mods to repair capacity. Arcane selection is a separate pass and final score is not surfaced as the actual damage metric.
- Status probabilities are weighted from quantized damage, but target status immunity is applied later during scoring without removing immune damage types and renormalizing the remaining proc weights.
- The damage calculator uses 1/32 quantization, average crit expectation, and average procs per shot. It does not simulate a full finite fight. It approximates status history and steady-state DoT; a separate clean-target direct-hit estimate now depletes Overguard, Shields, and Health in order across repeated mean-damage attacks.
- The current implementation now has dedicated damage, status-rules, target-damage, capacity, scaling, and enemy-profile fixtures, including independent damage examples and a small exhaustive legal-build oracle. Full stochastic encounter and representative global-quality validation are still absent.
- The current implementation runs optimization in a dedicated module Worker with progress and cancellation; this corrects the pre-change synchronous architecture described in the original audit.

### Verified current rules

- Damage 3.0 replaced Damage 2.0 material-based resistances with Health, Armor, Shields, and Overguard pools plus faction-specific damage type modifiers. Since Update 36, the Wiki lists one vulnerability as 1.5× and one resistance as 0.5×. Sub-factions have separate tables. [Official Update 36](https://www.warframe.com/en/patch-notes/psn/36-0-0), [current faction modifier table](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage_Type_Modifier)
- Standard enemy armor reduction through 2700 Net Armor is `0.90 × sqrt(armor / 2700)`, so the incoming damage multiplier is `1 - that value`. Level-scaled armor normally clamps to 200–2700 before strips. If exceptional Net Armor exceeds 2700, the Wiki specifies the older `armor / (armor + 300)` reduction branch. Armor does not reduce damage to Shields or Overguard. [Armor](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Armor), [Damage/Calculation](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Calculation)
- The current Damage Quantization page specifies 1/32 of Modded Base Damage with signed round-half-up behavior. Physical and elemental bonuses do not affect the scale; non-elemental bonuses such as base damage and faction modifiers multiply final quantized values without changing damage composition. Tenno-Hub's shared-scale application for uniform base-damage bonuses is algebraically equivalent; this rule now has independent composition/scaling fixtures. It has not been compared against in-game telemetry. [Damage Quantization](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage_Quantization), [Damage/Calculation](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Calculation)
- The current status table says the immune status types are removed before proc-weight renormalization; faction resistance/vulnerability does not change proc-type weights. Multishot creates additional independent chances, and chance above 100% allows multiple procs. Forced procs are separate from ordinary weighted status rolls. [Status Effect](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Status_Effect)
- The current Wiki describes the ten-stack Blast cap (four for bosses) and a `3 × sum(H)` cap/death radial that excludes the initially afflicted target, which receives each stack's `max(1, 0.3 × H)` payout. The Blast page was reviewed in the in-app browser on 2026-11-12; it shows a 2026-11-03 last-edit date and active-edit notice. The finite encounter now applies this radial through configured 0–20 same-profile neighbors' defenses and reports collateral separately from primary TTK. The optimizer estimates cap-radial value with Poisson proc arrivals but does not score death-triggered value. H/proc arrivals are expected-value estimates; neighbor status/position histories and chained deaths remain unsupported. [Damage/Calculation](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Calculation), [Blast Damage](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Blast_Damage)
- Tau vulnerability adds 10% received Status Chance per active Tau stack, to +100% at ten stacks; each stack lasts eight seconds. The finite weapon encounter applies this increase to later ordinary non-Tau status rolls only; Tau rolls and forced applications remain independent. Fractional stacks and proc arrivals are mean-field expectations. [Status Effect](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Status_Effect), [Status Chance](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Status_Chance)
- Current status effects include five-stack Impact and Puncture; ten-stack Cold, Blast, Corrosive, Gas, Magnetic, Radiation, Viral, and Tau; six-second damage statuses Slash, Heat, Electricity, Toxin, and Gas; Corrosive lasts eight seconds; Radiation lasts twelve; Blast has a 1.5-second detonation. Slash, Heat, and Toxin begin DoT ticks after one second; Electricity and Gas tick immediately. Heat gradually strips armor up to 50%; Corrosive strips 26% on the first stack and 6% per later stack up to 80%. Viral adds 100% health damage on its first stack, then 25% per later stack up to 325%. [Status Effect](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Status_Effect)
- Cold freezes an eligible target for three seconds at ten stacks and leaves three residual stacks afterward; active Overguard or boss caps can prevent reaching ten. The encounter implements the freeze and residual count; because the Wiki does not specify the residual stacks' remaining timers, Tenno-Hub gives them one fresh Cold duration. [Cold Damage](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Cold_Damage)
- Toxin normally bypasses Shields, with current documented exceptions including Treasurer and Hounds. Overguard is neutral to most damage types except Void's 1.5× vulnerability; armor does not mitigate it. Magnetic amplifies Shield and Overguard damage. These pools need separate handling in a target simulation. [Overguard](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Overguard), [Damage/Calculation](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Calculation)
- Standard Eximus have base 12 Overguard with a separate level curve. While Overguard remains, they resist crowd control, ignore Radiation procs, and accept at most four Cold stacks. The planner now applies the standard Eximus Health/Shield adjustments and uses standard armor scaling, as the Update 36 patch removed separate Eximus armor scaling. The expected encounter transitions Radiation eligibility and the Cold cap when Overguard breaks; special Eximus, Steel Path, and other post-Overguard interactions remain unsupported. [Eximus](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Eximus), [Enemy Level Scaling](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Enemy_Level_Scaling), [Official Update 36](https://www.warframe.com/en/patch-notes/psn/36-0-0)
- Critical damage above 100% chance has multiple tiers; the expected non-headshot multiplier `1 + critChance × (critMultiplier - 1)` is supported by the current Wiki average-damage formula. [Critical Hit](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Critical_Hit)
- The current Wiki encodes the weapon's base critical multiplier into 12 bits over the range 0–32, then decodes it before relative critical-damage bonuses are applied. The calculator now does this with binary32 intermediate arithmetic. [Critical Damage](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Critical_Hit)
- The current Damage/Calculation page specifies a `+1` accumulator for weapon DoT tick groups, binary32 intermediate operations, one faction multiplier on the proc seed and a second applicable faction multiplier afterward. It distinguishes independent Slash/Toxin stack ticks from consolidated Heat/Electricity/Gas tick groups. The calculator now follows those per-tick rules. [Damage/Calculation](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Damage/Calculation)
- Update 40.0.2 narrowed Damage Attenuation to true bosses and HUD-health-bar enemies; target identity is needed to model it. Update 43 added an in-game DoT preview across Health, Shields, and Overguard, reinforcing the need for pool-specific DoT calculation. [Official Update 40.0.2](https://www.warframe.com/en/patch-notes/pc/40-0-2), [Official Update 43](https://www.warframe.com/en/patch-notes/pc/43-0-0)

### Important uncertainties

- The Wiki's Damage/Calculation text is incomplete and its summary wording is less clear than the dedicated Damage Quantization page. The latter supplies the current order: quantize physical/elemental contributions at Modded Base Damage/32, then multiply by non-elemental bonuses. This is documented as Wiki-verified but not telemetry-verified.
- Remaining DoT/status approximations include the expected critical-seed distribution, forced-proc occurrence probability, and Poisson estimate for consolidated tick-group activity. The finite mean-value simulator schedules supported Slash/Heat/Electricity/Toxin/Gas ticks and duration-scaled Blast fuses; it pays each individual stack's `max(1, 0.3 × H)` to the host and applies configured cap/death radial damage to nearby same-profile pools. The optimizer retains the expected single-target payout rate and adds a Poisson cap-radial estimate, but not death-triggered radial value. Same-source Heat stacks share a refreshed tick stream. Cross-source Heat inheritance, consolidated Electricity/Gas histories, neighbor status/position histories, and Blast chain deaths remain unsupported.
- A faction preset is not a specific enemy. Armor/health/shield values and damage attenuation vary per enemy and level; current synthetic presets cannot support exact target-specific TTK claims.
- Custom current-level pool inputs, ten sourced ordinary-unit profiles, and a standard Eximus toggle are available. The finite mean-value encounter schedules supported DoTs, tracks expected Heat armor-strip timing plus Viral, Corrosive, Magnetic, Cold, Puncture, and Tau stacks, and resolves duration-scaled Blast fuses and single-target stack payouts. Configured Blast neighbors receive cap/death radial damage through their own defenses, reported apart from primary TTK. Active Tau stacks increase later ordinary non-Tau status rolls. Same-source Heat stack refresh is modeled; cross-source inheritance remains open. The finite encounter and ordered direct-hit sequence apply the user-selected generic Shield Gate mode; the one-hit estimate/optimizer, shield regeneration, neighbor statuses, positions, chained deaths, other post-Overguard interactions, boss attenuation, random outcomes, and broad enemy-specific exceptions remain unsupported. The encounter remains fractional and mean-field; optimizer scoring is a separate steady-state pool-time heuristic.
- Current weapon, mod, arcane, and passive coverage is not yet fully audited against the current data sources.

### HiGHS / MILP evaluation

The current JavaScript package (`highs`, maintained as `highs-js`) exposes LP, mixed-integer linear, and convex quadratic models in Node and modern browsers. Its WebAssembly loader is asynchronous; solver calls are synchronous after loading, so the package documentation recommends running non-trivial solves in a Worker. The documented WASM build is single-threaded and requires explicit `locateFile` handling when the bundler does not emit `highs.wasm` beside the loader. Persistent models require explicit disposal to release their WASM allocations. These API constraints were verified in a disposable `highs@1.15.4` Node prototype on 2026-11-14; the model and runtime results are recorded in the latest follow-up. [HiGHS project](https://highs.dev/), [highs-js package documentation](https://github.com/lovasoa/highs-js), [API reference](https://lovasoa.github.io/highs-js/docs/)

The Build Planner's present objective is not a linear or convex quadratic function of independent mod-selection variables. Element combination depends on mod-slot order; damage is quantized; crit tiers, status weighting, conditionals, target pool routing, armor/status state, and magazine cadence introduce discrete and nonlinear interactions. A MILP could choose among precomputed legal candidate builds or linearized interaction features, then send finalists through the exact evaluator, but that would be a candidate selector, not a direct exact model of the current game rules. Modeling the full current objective as a useful MILP would require a large set of order, synergy, and state variables, and it would still need final domain evaluation.

The existing worker-side architecture—capacity-constrained candidate generation, current damage evaluation, and nonlinear local refinement—is a practical hybrid for this model and preserves exact legality checks. The current six-case proxy comparison shows improvement over the previous repository search, and bounded exact oracles match the finite Expected TTK result across five attack pools. The HiGHS runtime prototype solved only an additive surrogate and is not evidence of actual build quality. HiGHS has not been added as a runtime dependency; the solver-choice acceptance item remains open until a damage-informed candidate formulation can be compared on solution quality and browser cost.

## Overwhelming Attrition finite stack progression (2026-11-13)

When the timer ends, one stack is removed and the timer resets. The current parser now preserves this trigger, duration, cap, and additive damage behavior for supported Incarnon attacks.

The calculator reports a Poisson time-average stack estimate for long-run DPS. Expected TTK uses a separate finite path: it removes the steady-state damage bonus from the first-hit stats, starts with the selected initial stack state, evolves stack-count/timer probability states after each qualifying attack group, and applies each attack's current expected damage bracket and quantization. Reaching the cap refreshes the timer; an expiry removes one stack. This avoids applying three steady-state stacks to the first shot of a cold encounter. The UI trace shows the qualifying attack-group probability and distinguishes steady-state from finite behavior.

### HiGHS solver fit review (2026-11-14)

The current HiGHS JavaScript package (`highs` 1.15.4 as listed by npm on this review date) compiles to WebAssembly for Node and modern browsers. Its published model classes are LP, mixed-integer linear programming, and convex QP; the package documentation explicitly says it does not solve general nonlinear models or mixed-integer quadratic programs. Sources: [highs-js README](https://github.com/lovasoa/highs-js/blob/main/README.md), [HiGHS solver overview](https://ergo-code.github.io/HiGHS/dev/solvers/), [npm package](https://www.npmjs.com/package/highs).

The planner's hard legality rules can be linearized with binary variables for mod/rank/slot selection, incompatibility groups, and capacity. Its authoritative objective cannot: elemental order and combination, critical-tier quantization, conditional uptime, status distribution, armor and shield routing, discrete pool depletion, and scheduled DoT events are evaluated by nonlinear damage functions and a finite event simulator. A HiGHS objective would therefore be a surrogate unless it encoded a large discretized state model. Pairwise linearization would still omit higher-order elemental/status synergies and encounter timing. The implemented hybrid instead includes capacity feasibility during beam construction, uses a fast damage proxy to broaden candidate search, and reranks Expected TTK candidates through the exact finite evaluator. Curated exhaustive legal-subset and benchmark evidence supports this design, but does not prove full-catalog optimality or establish a direct HiGHS quality win.

A temporary `npm install --no-save --no-package-lock --ignore-scripts highs@1.15.4` attempt produced no output for over a minute in the restricted shell and was cancelled; it changed no declared dependency. This means no solver/browser latency or surrogate-quality head-to-head was measured. Do not claim the hybrid is empirically superior to HiGHS yet. A direct comparison remains open: implement a small linearized selector only in a benchmark branch, compare its exact-scored legal outputs to the current hybrid across matched candidate pools, and measure the same production Worker path before deciding whether any solver component earns runtime inclusion.
